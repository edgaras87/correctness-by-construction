# Approach evaluation

**On trial.** How an Approach on Trial is judged, and its pieces
placed where they belong. The Concept Deliverer's alone: a CBC Run hands its
Approaches on Trial over and never evaluates. The part a CBC Run holds is
`.claude/approaches-on-trial/approach-drafting.md`.

## What it is for

So that what a piece of work taught goes where it belongs, piece by
piece, and nothing unproven is locked into a convention. Before
this, first-time work's close made one lasting form, chosen at once.
The redesign of the conventions manual, on 2026-10-19, closed as
three pieces, which one form could not say. The refinement's draft was counted as a
manual before anyone judged whether it was one.

## The life of an Approach on Trial

1. **Drafted:** during its first use, specific to that work, by
   the approach-drafting Approach on Trial.
2. **On trial:** rewritten in general at its first evaluation. Each
   later use adds a dated note and is evaluated again.
3. **Placed or dropped:** a piece is placed into a convention when
   it meets what its Approach on Trial said would settle it. A piece
   can be dropped at any evaluation. The Approach on Trial's file goes when nothing is left in it.

## When it opens

- **A drafted Approach on Trial is handed over.** Ours, at the close of
  the approach-drafting Approach on Trial. A CBC Run's, when a CBC Run Reading meets its offer line
  under *To the deliverer*.
- **An Approach on Trial has a new dated note** from a later use,
  ours or a CBC Run's.

Placing is not a third moment: it is the "placed" ending, reached at
either.

## Stages

1. **Read the Approach on Trial whole,** and its history: one commit per
   lesson, against what the work did. For a CBC Run's, the commits its
   offer line names.
2. **Split it into pieces,** one subject each: a procedure, a rule,
   a form of output, a fact.
3. **Find each piece's home.** An existing convention first: a
   section of its manual, or a section to add to it. A new
   convention only when none fits. A fact may belong in a record.
4. **Propose an ending for each piece,** with the reason. The
   reviewer decides each:
   - **placed:** written into its home now;
   - **on trial:** kept, and rewritten in general;
   - **the project's own:** it stays with the project that drafted
     it, and goes no further;
   - **dropped:** nothing in it is worth keeping.

   For a CBC Run's Approach on Trial, each ending is also one of the Exchange Convention's
   three verdicts (`docs/conventions/exchange/` §6.5, *Verdicts*): placed is
   taken, on trial is held, the project's own and dropped are
   declined.
5. **Place what is placed,** as a commit plan. A new convention is added as the conventions manual says
   (`docs/conventions/conventions/` §3, *Adding a convention*). A section of an
   existing one goes into its manual first, and the Foundation
   Check carries it to what derives from it.
6. **Rewrite what stays on trial, in general.** The general
   statement comes first, in its own terms. Each use stands beside
   it as a dated note, the evidence its next evaluation reads. The Approach on Trial names what
   would settle it. If a whole part is used by one Convention Seat alone, it
   splits (below).
7. **Record the endings.** For our own Approach on Trial, an ADR when a
   convention is made or changed and options were weighed. For a
   CBC Run's, the CBC Run Reading's Delivery Note carries each ending.

## Seats

- **An Approach on Trial splits by Convention Seat only when a whole part is used by one
  Convention Seat alone.** That part becomes an Approach on Trial of its own, and the
  other Convention Seat never receives it. This one split from
  the approach-drafting Approach on Trial that way.
- **A step that differs is marked inline** in the shared Approach on Trial:
  "At the Concept Deliverer:", "In a CBC Run:". Not "here", which means the
  reader's own Convention Seat.
- **Each idea lives in the part that acts on it.** The Approach on Trial Stages and
  the Convention Seats are here because only an evaluation acts on them.
- **Our part points at the shared one by its path.** The shared one
  says the Concept Deliverer holds a part that does not ship, and gives no
  path: a CBC Run cannot open it.
- What ships to a CBC Run is the Exchange Convention's
  (`docs/conventions/exchange/` §3.2, *What goes*).

## The reviewer's reading is the check

This Approach on Trial judges Approaches on Trial, itself and the approach-drafting Approach on Trial
among them. The agent will tend to find its own Approach on Trial good. So
the agent proposes, the reviewer decides each ending, and no
evaluation closes on the agent's word.

## Not known yet

- **Whether a manual still goes on trial.** A convention could be
  adopted marked on trial, as first-time work was. Now unproven work
  stays in Approaches on Trial, and the mark may no longer be needed.
- **Whether each Approach on Trial's *Settled when* is enough,** or placing
  needs a set number of uses first.
- **Whether this and the approach-drafting Approach on Trial become one convention**
  when placed: one manual, an Agent Skill for each Convention Seat.
- **What a CBC Run does with a piece left as the project's own:** keeps
  it as its Approach on Trial, or writes it as an Agent Skill or Agent Rule of
  its own (`knowledge/backbone-ideas/agent-files.md` §7, *Agent Skills and Agent Rules*).
- **Whether generalizing is work of its own.** Today it is Stage 6.
  Both are the Concept Deliverer's, so no Convention Seat splits them; a first use
  that shows it big enough splits it.
- **How a CBC Run's offered Approach on Trial reaches this evaluation.** Only
  *When it opens* says so. Weighed on 2026-10-20: an Agent Skill opening
  when a CBC Run Reading meets the offer line; the working-a-reading Agent Rule
  pointing here; or nothing, the CBC Run Reading working it by hand.

It has evaluated an Approach on Trial of ours and one of a CBC Run's, placed a
